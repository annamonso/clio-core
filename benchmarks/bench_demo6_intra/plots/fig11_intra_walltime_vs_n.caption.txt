Wall time of one `claude -p` planner invocation that issues N parallel Bash tool calls per LLM turn (each call: `sleep 1; echo`). N ∈ {1,4,10}, n=3 runs per level on a single ares-comp-13 host, 60 s pacing between runs. Measured points (blue, median±stdev with individual runs) sit on the parallel-baseline projection (green dashed: +1 s flat) rather than the serial-baseline projection (red dashed: +(N−1)·1 s). The N=10 measured residual vs the N=1 baseline is 2.93 s — well below the 9 s a serialized dispatch would have added. Conclusion: Claude Code's harness dispatches multiple `tool_use` blocks in a single assistant message concurrently. The small linear-in-N residual above the 1 s parallel floor is consistent with a fixed per-tool dispatch + result-aggregation overhead in the harness.
